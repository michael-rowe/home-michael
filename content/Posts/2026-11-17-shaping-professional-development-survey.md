> [!info] I want Path to help with the learning as well as the record
> Most people care about their professional development but find recording it to be a chore. I'm building Path to make the record easier and to help with the development behind it, and I want to know what works for you. The survey takes about five minutes and is open until 18 December 2026.

I want Path to be a great continuing professional development (CPD) tool. That means it's quick to add to when something happens, easy to search when you need something again, and able to put together what your regulator, professional body or employer asks for without you losing a weekend to get it done. I also want it to help with the part that comes before the record: working out where you'd like to go next, and noticing what your everyday work is teaching you along the way. If it does both, development and career progression become something you keep doing through the year, and the record accumulates as you go.

So far I've built Path mostly from my own experience and from conversations with a handful of people, which only gets me so far. I'd like to hear from you about what works, what doesn't, and what you'd want a tool like this to do.

## What people find hardest about CPD

I've been reading what's been written about how professionals experience CPD, mostly in nursing, medicine and the allied health professions, and the same complaints come up again and again. Most people say their development matters to them. The trouble is usually everything around it:

- **Recording takes longer than it should.** Log in, find the certificate, upload it, fill in the form. Some people leave it all until the last few months before a deadline.
- **Getting proof is hard.** An in-house session might have nothing more than a sign-in sheet, and chasing a certificate can take several emails.
- **Writing reflections is the slowest part.** Talking something through with a colleague is often the part people value most, and writing it up is the part they like least.
- **Everything happens around the deadline.** People seek feedback and reflect more as their renewal date gets closer, and drift back once it's passed.
- **It doesn't fit an unusual career.** Parental leave, roles away from direct practice and working on your own all sit awkwardly in rules written for a typical career, and moving to a new employer can mean repeating training you've already done.

I recognise most of these from my own career as an academic, where promotion and fellowship applications tend to get pulled together in a rush from years of work that was never kept with them in mind.

## Tell me what works for you

The survey takes about five minutes and it's anonymous. It asks what you use to record your development, what prompts you to work on it, what you like about how you do it now, what gets in the way, and what you'd want a tool to do for you. It's open to anyone in any profession, including people who don't keep a record at all.

When you finish, you can download *Everyday development*, a short guide to learning from the work you already do.

The survey closes on 18 December 2026, and I'll share what I learn after that. I'd be really grateful if you passed this on in your own networks, as I'd love to get as many perspectives as possible.
