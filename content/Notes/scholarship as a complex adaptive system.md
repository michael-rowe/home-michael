> [!info] Scholarship and professional education as sociotechnical complex adaptive systems
> People, institutions, technologies, and now AI agents interact in ways that produce system behaviour nobody specified. Managing that kind of system as though it were complicated, by writing procedures and auditing compliance, suppresses the adaptation it runs on. This note sets out the lens, the tensions it makes visible, what AI agents add, and the opposite mistake of using complexity as cover.

## Scholarship as a complex adaptive system

**One-sentence definition:** A lens that treats education and scholarship as systems of interdependent social and technical agents whose behaviour emerges from their interactions, rather than as machines whose outputs follow from their inputs.

This is the theoretical grounding underneath [[Notes/emergent scholarship|emergent scholarship]], and it earns its place by making a prediction the managerial account can't: that procedures will keep failing in the same way, because the system is the wrong kind of thing to write procedures for.

"Sociotechnical" matters more than the softer "ecosystem", because it treats technologies as agents in the system. A reference manager, an assessment platform, an AI tutor: each changes what is easy to do, and so what gets done, and so what the system comes to treat as normal.

## Why the system can't be specified

Complicated systems can be specified and complex ones can't, for reasons that are structural: interactions are non-linear, so effort and effect come apart; boundaries are open, so you can't say what is in the system; behaviour depends on history, so the same intervention does different things at different moments; and agents respond to local conditions in ignorance of the whole. See [[Notes/emergence|emergence]].

Every quality assurance instrument in higher education assumes the opposite. Learning outcomes are declared in advance, research questions fixed at ethics approval, impact projected in a funding application, and the curriculum mapped as though the mapping were the thing. Each is a reasonable request to make of a complicated system and an impossible one to make of a complex system, so what gets produced is a document that satisfies the request while the real work proceeds somewhere else. Nobody is being dishonest. It is what happens when you audit a rainforest with a parts list.

Eoyang and Mennin (2019) make the same point from inside health professions education. Every institution is different, every clinical setting unique, every student starting somewhere else, "and yet, we focus on the standardization, rigor, and accountability to competencies and curricula in every facet." Their conclusion is the one that matters here: *the search for consistency and control in such a highly variable system leads inevitably to intractable, wicked problems.* The wickedness is manufactured by the mismatch between the instrument and the system.

[[Notes/wicked problems|Wicked problems]], in Rittel and Webber's (1973) sense, have no definitive formulation, no stopping rule, and solutions that are good or bad rather than true or false. What follows is a different kind of action: contextualised, iterative, incremental moves that shift a persistent pattern over time.

## The tensions worth naming

These are the sites where the complexity gets negotiated in practice. They are where the system's adaptation comes from, and the productive management of tension, in place of its resolution, is what generates anything durable (Davis & Sumara, 2005).

**Standardisation against personalisation.** Accreditation demands comparability; meaningful education requires adaptation to this learner in this context.

**Efficiency against relationship.** Technology promises scale, but learning is relational. The question is which feedback loops correct for what is lost, and whether any exist (Selwyn, 2011).

**Control against emergence.** Policy assumes predictability; learning is emergent and largely invisible to the instruments pointed at it.

**Regulation against innovation.** Regulation secures a floor and at the same time locks behaviour in, which is why innovation tends to appear at the periphery and has to travel inward.

**Transmission against transformation.** Is education the transfer of stable knowledge, or the creation of conditions from which understanding and professional identity emerge? The two answers imply incompatible curricula and incompatible assessment (Laurillard, 2012).

## What AI agents add

A tool does what it is told. An agent acts. AI agents acquire novel behaviours through interaction with their environment and with each other, and those behaviours are effectively impossible to predict; even when a solution can be described mathematically, the description can be too long and too tangled to interpret. This is why Rahwan (2019) argues they need studying as a class of actors with their own behavioural patterns, alongside their study as artefacts of engineering.

Adding them to scholarship adds agents, interactions, and feedback loops to a system that was already complex, and three things follow. The system becomes less predictable, which is a real cost and should be said plainly. It also becomes more generative, since more interaction is more opportunity for emergence, and some of what comes back is good in ways nobody could have specified. And the locus of control shifts: you can't specify an agent's outputs in advance, but you can bound the context it operates within, what it can see, how it may reason, what it may do without asking. That is the move the [[Notes/research harness|research harness]] makes, and it is a response suited to a complex system where a compliance regime would be suited to a complicated one.

## The opposite failure

Everything above argues against treating a complex system as complicated. The mirror error is just as available and, in this literature, more common: reaching for complexity to explain why something couldn't be planned, measured, or accounted for, when the analysis simply wasn't done.

Complexity language is easy to use decoratively. "The system is complex" can excuse any absence of evidence, any unevaluated intervention, any claim that resists checking. It can also be used to dodge the parts of education that genuinely are tractable, such as whether a curriculum covers what it claims, whether an assessment measures what it says, or whether an intervention outperformed the alternative. Those are answerable questions and complexity is no reason to stop answering them.

Cristancho, Field, and Lingard (2019) documented this. They found complexity becoming a "god term" in medical education, and identified four patterns across eighteen years of published work: complexity described in a variety of incompatible ways, multiple orientations combined inside a single paper, the orientation in use left implicit, and citation through secondary sources in place of primary ones. Their argument is that there are several legitimate complexity sciences with distinct disciplinary roots, and that using the word without saying which one you mean produces the appearance of theory.

The test is whether the lens constrains you. A framework that explains every outcome equally well after the fact explains nothing, and complexity is unusually prone to this. If invoking it never rules anything out, it is being used as cover.

## What to do in place of planning

Nudge and watch. Change what is easy, change what is visible, change who is in the room, and observe what comes back. Small experiments in contained contexts, documented openly, do better than institutional strategies that assume the destination is knowable (Bleakley & Cleland, 2022).

Tolerating mess is part of the same discipline. Half-formed material that hasn't been filed is the substrate emergence works on, and premature organisation forecloses the connections you hadn't thought of yet. Systems that demand everything be categorised on arrival optimise for retrieval and starve discovery.

### What remains unclear

Where the boundary falls in a given case, between a complex system and a complicated one you haven't analysed hard enough. Stacey's (1996) matrix is one instrument for this, locating a situation by how much agreement and how much certainty are available and distinguishing simple, complicated, complex, and chaotic zones with different appropriate responses. It is a useful discipline, though it asks you to judge certainty in advance, which is close to the thing being disputed.

The larger gap is a test: what does this lens forecast that a managerial account does not, and did it happen? The prediction above about procedures failing in the same way is a start. It hasn't been checked properly.
