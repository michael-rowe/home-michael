> [!info] A rocket is complicated; a rainforest is complex
> Emergence is the appearance of properties at the level of a whole that are not present in any of its constituent parts. It is the reason some systems can be taken apart and understood, and others cannot. It is also a reason for why writing out the steps of a process is sometimes an accurate description and sometimes a fiction.

## Emergence

**One-sentence definition:** A property is emergent when it appears through the interaction of elements that do not individually possess it, and cannot be predicted from those elements considered separately.

Water is wet. Neither hydrogen nor oxygen is wet. Wetness is not hiding in the molecules waiting to be found; it appears when enough of them interact. An ant has a small repertoire of general-purpose behaviours and no idea what a nest is. The colony builds nests, forages and defends itself. The colony's competence is not distributed across the ants in smaller portions; it exists only in their interaction.

## Complicated is not complex

A rocket is complicated. It has an enormous number of parts, and understanding it is hard, but it is decomposable: the parts can be specified, the interactions are known, and the behaviour of the whole follows from the behaviour of the parts. Given enough expertise you can write down the steps.

A rainforest is complex. You cannot specify it by listing its components, because what it does arises from interactions that are non-linear, historically contingent and continuously changing. There is no set of steps that adequately describe an end-state or target-state of a rainforest.

The distinction matters because the two call for opposite responses. Complicated systems reward planning, specification and control. Applying the same treatment to a complex system suppresses the local adaptation the system runs on, and produces an elaborate plan that describes something other than what is happening.

## Diagnosis is the skill

Neither category is the better one, and complexity is a property of particular systems rather than a condition the world is in. Most real work involves both, often in the same afternoon.

Getting the diagnosis wrong is a mistake in both directions. Treat a complex system as complicated and you produce procedures nobody can follow and plans that describe a fiction. Treat a complicated system as complex and you abandon work that was tractable, excuse yourself from rigour you could have exercised, and mistake "nobody can predict this" for a finding rather than a failure to do the analysis.

Rockets still reach Mars. Drug trials still establish what works. A great deal can be predicted with high certainty, and where it can be, it should be. The claim that some systems resist specification is not a claim that specification is generally futile — it is an argument for spending the effort where it pays and recognising where it does not.

The Stacey matrix is one tool for making that call. It locates a situation by two dimensions — how much agreement exists about what to do, and how much certainty exists about cause and effect — producing four zones. Close to both, the situation is simple and past data predicts the future; plan and monitor against the plan. Far from one but not the other, it is complicated, and the work is either political (negotiating agreement) or exploratory (holding a shared direction while the path stays open). Far from both, it is complex, and what it needs is adaptability rather than better planning. Further still lies chaos. The matrix does not remove the judgement, but it makes it explicit rather than instinctive.

## Features that make a system complex

- Many interacting elements, exchanging matter or information
- Rich interaction: everything is affected by, and affects, other things
- Non-linearity: small changes can produce large effects, and large efforts nothing at all
- Feedback: any interaction can loop back on itself
- Open or undefinable boundaries — it is often impossible to say where the system ends
- Operation far from equilibrium; organisation requires continuous energy
- History-dependence: the system's past is co-responsible for its present behaviour
- Local ignorance: elements respond to what is near them, not to the system as a whole

Each of these defeats a different part of the planning instinct. Together they mean the behaviour of the whole is available only by watching it, not by calculating it.

## Why this shows up in knowledge work

Knowledge is an emergent property of interaction. Understanding arrives through dialogue and exploration rather than through the transfer of a finished object — which is why a lecture and a conversation about the same content produce different results, and why a reader alone with a text can question an assumption but not test it.

The same logic applies one level up, to how scholarly contributions come into being. See [[Notes/emergent scholarship|emergent scholarship]].
