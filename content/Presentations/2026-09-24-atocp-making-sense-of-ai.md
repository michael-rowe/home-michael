## Overview

The ATOCP session at this year's BOA Congress is called *Fearless futures: challenging culture and navigating AI with confidence*, and I think the fear it names is reasonable. The talk starts from it, with the head-to-head studies from 2023 and 2024 in which AI diagnosed more accurately than doctors and wrote replies to patients that clinicians rated as more empathetic than the doctors' own.

From there it looks at what current tools can do in clinical and physiotherapy practice, and at four common responses to them, which I call sanctuary strategies: denial, retreat, restriction, and resignation. Each of them leaves the clinician watching something happen to them. What I'd offer instead is that physiotherapists hold something AI systems don't. The models have no memory of you between sessions, know nothing of your field since they were trained, and have no idea who your patients are, so what makes their output useful in a clinic is the context a clinician brings to it: your specialism, your patient population, local services and pathways, how you reason, and what you've seen work.

The second half turns to patients, who are already using AI to research diagnoses and prepare questions before they arrive. Context sovereignty applies to them too. Their own AI agents will carry their history and goals into the consultation, and the talk asks what that does to the therapeutic relationship, and to the MDT, when everyone in the room brings their own context.

The session ends with three things to do the next working day: write down your professional context, explicitly enough to give to an AI; give one patient a structured prompt to use before their next appointment; and read the next AI output you meet as you would a colleague's opinion.
