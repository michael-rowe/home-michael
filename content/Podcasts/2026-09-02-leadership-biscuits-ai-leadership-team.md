**Leadership Biscuits** · Hosted by Sharon Black · 2 September 2026 · 42 min

*Recorded July 2026*

Sharon Black invited me onto *Leadership Biscuits* to work through a question her listeners keep running into: what does AI actually ask of the people running health and social care organisations? The short answer is that it asks more of them than delegation. AI reaches into decision-making, workflow, governance, education and patient care, which puts it outside the remit of any single department.

## What we discussed

- Why AI behaves differently from previous waves of technology change, and why that makes it a leadership problem rather than an IT procurement one
- Using AI as a thinking partner — something to argue with about a decision — rather than as a faster way to produce documents
- Whether AI will replace jobs in healthcare, and why augmentation describes what is actually happening more accurately
- What changes when patients arrive at an appointment having already consulted a capable system
- Why universities need to rethink assessment rather than defend it
- How AI exposes the inefficient processes and bottlenecks an organisation had learned to live with
- Why a culture of experimentation beats a culture of fear, and what leaders have to do to create one
- The trap of measuring AI adoption by how much AI people use
- Why leaders need first-hand experience of these systems rather than a consultant's summary
- What an organisation built around intelligence as a service might look like

The thread running underneath all of it is whether we are preparing our organisations, and the people in them, for the world that is actually arriving rather than the one we planned for. My own view is that leaders cannot get there by talking about AI. They have to use it, watch where it fails, and find out for themselves where it adds value.

I also said, and still mean, that the most interesting version of this conversation is the one we have in five years, when we can see how much of it we got wrong.
